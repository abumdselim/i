{{I:compass}} Why we work this way

Small enough to be accountable.

Large agencies sell a team you meet once and a document you cannot use without them. We have seen how that ends: a system that works, a client who cannot change it, and an invoice that never quite stops.

So we stay small and senior. One build per lead, an architecture written before the first line of code, and a handover that leaves your team independent. It limits how quickly we can grow. It is also why clients keep the systems we build.

Our work has one thing in common: the software sits close to the money, the stock or the customer record. That is not a place for experiments dressed as products.

{{I:flag}} Principles

Four positions we do not trade.

These are working rules, not values on a wall. They decide what gets built, what gets cut and when we say no.

{{I:layers}}P/01

Architecture before tooling

The stack is the last decision, not the first. We model the domain, then choose the smallest set of tools that can carry it.

{{I:approval}}P/02

Every automation needs an exit

A human checkpoint, an audit trail and a switch that turns the feature off without taking the system down.

{{I:database}}P/03

Boring where it counts

Postgres, queues, logs, backups. Reliability is the part of the product nobody demos and everybody notices.

{{I:doc}}P/04

Handover is part of the build

Documentation, runbooks and a recorded walkthrough ship with the software, not after it, and not as an upsell.

{{I:ban}} Boundaries

What we don't do.

  • {{I:close}}Sell staff by the head without owning the outcome.
  • {{I:close}}Ship an automated decision that nobody can review.
  • {{I:close}}Lock a client into our hosting, our licence or our accounts.
  • {{I:close}}Take a build we cannot staff with people you have already met.
  • {{I:close}}Quote a number before we have read the system.

{{I:link}} Working together

Visible from the first week.

An engagement starts with a short call and a written scope. From there we work in public: a shared repository, a visible board, and working software demoed at the end of every increment. You always know what is built, what is next and what is at risk.

We keep the team stable across the engagement. The person who designs the architecture is on the call when it goes to production, and the same people write the handover.
